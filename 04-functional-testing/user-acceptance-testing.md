## Overview

User Acceptance Testing (UAT) is a type of Functional Testing performed to verify that the software satisfies business requirements and is ready for production use.

Unlike other testing activities that focus primarily on identifying defects, UAT evaluates whether the application supports real-world business processes and meets the expectations of its intended users.

UAT is typically the final testing phase before production deployment.

## Objective

The objective of User Acceptance Testing (UAT) is to validate that the software fulfills business requirements, supports real-world user workflows, and satisfies the agreed acceptance criteria before release.

---

## Common Verification Areas

User Acceptance Testing commonly verifies:

* Business workflows
* User acceptance criteria
* Functional requirements
* Data accuracy
* Business rules
* User experience from a business perspective

---

## Key Deliverables

| Activity            | Deliverable                    |

| UAT Execution       | UAT Results                    |
| Business Validation | User Acceptance Sign-off       |
| Failed Acceptance   | Bug Reports or Change Requests |

---

## Benefits

* Confirms business requirements have been met.
* Validates real-world business processes.
* Builds stakeholder confidence before deployment.
* Reduces the risk of production issues.
* Provides formal approval for software release.

---

## Best Practices

#### Planning

* Define acceptance criteria early.
* Involve representative business users.
* Prepare realistic business scenarios.

#### Execution

* Execute real-world business workflows.
* Record user feedback and observations.
* Document issues that prevent business acceptance.

---

## In Practice

A company completes development of an employee management system.

Before deployment, representatives from the Human Resources department perform User Acceptance Testing by creating employee records, approving leave requests, updating employee information, and generating reports using realistic business scenarios.

After confirming that the system supports their daily operations and satisfies the agreed acceptance criteria, the business representatives formally approve the application for production release.

---

## Developer Tips

* Clarify acceptance criteria before development begins.
* Implement features according to business requirements.
* Support QA and business users during UAT.
* Address accepted defects and change requests promptly.
* Remember that technical correctness does not always guarantee business acceptance.

---

## Common Mistakes

* Treating UAT as another round of System Testing.
* Allowing only technical team members to perform UAT.
* Beginning UAT without defined acceptance criteria.
* Ignoring business user feedback.
* Assuming successful Functional Testing automatically guarantees business acceptance.

---

## Summary

User Acceptance Testing (UAT) verifies that software satisfies business requirements and supports real-world user workflows before production deployment.

By involving business stakeholders in the final validation process, UAT provides confidence that the application is ready for operational use and meets the needs of its intended users.
